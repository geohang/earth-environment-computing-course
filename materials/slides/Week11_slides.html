Foundation

Spatial Autocorrelation

  • Tobler (1970): near things are more related than distant things
  • Quantify it: how different are pairs, as distance grows?
  • Close pairs differ little; distant pairs differ up to a ceiling
  • 80 points make 3,160 pairs: the raw material
SEES:4100 | Week 11 | 5
The Semivariogram

Half the Squared Difference, Binned

gamma(h) = mean of 0.5 * (z_i - z_j)^2 for pairs at distance h
z_i, z_j = values at two locations
h = separation distance (binned)
gamma = semivariance at that distance
small gamma = the pair still resembles
  • One curve turns 3,160 pair comparisons into a spatial story
SEES:4100 | Week 11 | 6
The Semivariogram

Three Features Carry the Science

Nugget

Intercept near zero distance: measurement noise plus variation finer than the sampling.

Sill

The plateau: the field's overall variance. The resemblance budget runs out.

Range

Distance where the curve reaches the sill. Beyond it, points stop informing each other.

IDW

Inverse Distance Weighting

z_hat = sum(w_i * z_i) / sum(w_i), w_i = 1 / d_i^p
z_hat = predicted value at the target
d_i = distance to observation i
p = the power parameter
at an observation: return it exactly
  • A weighted average where distance sets the weights
SEES:4100 | Week 11 | 9
IDW

What the Power Does

p near 0

Everything weighs equally: the field mean, everywhere. Maximum smoothing.

p = 2

The convention, not a law. Balanced borrowing from neighbors.

p large

Only the nearest point counts: nearest-neighbor patches, bullseyes on the map.

The lab draws the same field at p = 1, 2, 4 and lets validation pick.
SEES:4100 | Week 11 | 10
IDW

Twelve Lines, the Whole Idea

def idw(x_target, power):
    distance = np.abs(x_obs - x_target)
    if np.any(distance == 0):
        return values[np.argmin(distance)]
    weights = 1.0 / distance**power
    return np.sum(weights * values) / np.sum(weights)

for p in [0.5, 2, 8]:
    print(p, idw(5.0, p))
  • Watch the prediction drift toward the nearest observation as p grows

Validation

Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation

1

Remove

Take one observation out of the dataset.

2

Predict

Interpolate its location from the remaining 79.

3

Compare

Prediction versus the held-out truth.

4

Repeat

All 80 points; the RMSE is the map's honest error.

SEES:4100 | Week 11 | 12
Validation

What LOO Does and Does Not Say

  • Scores the method AT observation locations
  • Far from all observations, the map is extrapolating
  • LOO is silent there: mask or caveat those areas
  • The 1:1 scatter shows compression: highs under, lows over

Beyond IDW

What Kriging Adds (and Why We Stop Here)

  • Kriging picks weights FROM the variogram, not from a fixed power
  • It attaches an uncertainty map to the prediction map
  • The price: fitting a variogram model and heavier assumptions
  • IDW plus honest validation covers this course's needs
