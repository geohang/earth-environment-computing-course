Concepts

Seven measures, each worked by hand on five numbers.

Part 1 · Concept 1 of 7

Mean and Median

Definition

The mean is the sum divided by the count. The median is the middle value once sorted.

Equation

x̄ = (1/n) Σ xᵢ

median = middle value,
         once sorted

Five days of flow, hundreds of cfs

Sorted:  2, 3, 3, 4, 18

x̄      = (2+3+3+4+18) / 5
       = 30 / 5 = 6

median = 3rd of 5 sorted = 3
One flood day pulls the mean up; the median stays at 3. Without it, both are 3.
Part 1 · Concept 2 of 7

Skew and the Log Transform

Definition

Skew measures how lopsided values are: 0 is symmetric, positive means a long right tail. log10 turns equal ratios into equal steps.

Equation

g = (1/n) Σ ((xᵢ − x̄) / s)³

z = log₁₀ x   ⇔   x = 10ᶻ

Flow that doubles each step

Raw:    100, 200, 400, 800, 1600 cfs
        g = 0.89

log₁₀:  2.0, 2.3, 2.6, 2.9, 3.2
        g = 0

+0.30 in log₁₀ = ×2 in cfs;  +1 = ×10
Same five values. On the raw axis the gaps grow; in log10 they are equal.
Part 1 · Concept 3 of 7

Correlation, r

Definition

r measures how closely points fall along one straight line, from −1 to +1. It says nothing about why.

Equation

      Σ (xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)
r = ──────────────────────────
    √( Σ(xᵢ−x̄)² · Σ(yᵢ−ȳ)² )

Five weeks of rain (x) and flow (y)

x = 1, 2, 3, 4, 5
y = 1, 3, 2, 5, 4       x̄ = ȳ = 3

Σ(x−x̄)(y−ȳ) = 4+0+0+2+2 = 8
Σ(x−x̄)² = 10    Σ(y−ȳ)² = 10

r = 8 / √(10 × 10) = 0.80
Most points sit upper-right or lower-left of the means, so the products are positive.
Part 1 · Concept 4 of 7

Least Squares

Definition

A residual is observed minus predicted. Least squares picks the line with the smallest sum of squared residuals, SSE.

Equation

eᵢ  = yᵢ − ŷᵢ

SSE = Σ eᵢ²   →   minimum

Same five points, line ŷ = 0.6 + 0.8x

Residuals:
  −0.4, +0.8, −1.0, +1.2, −0.6

SSE = 0.16+0.64+1.00+1.44+0.36
    = 3.60

Every other straight line gives a larger SSE
Residuals are vertical because we predict y from x. Squaring makes one big miss count more than several small ones.
Part 1 · Concept 5 of 7

Slope and Intercept

Definition

The slope is the change in y for one more unit of x. The intercept is the predicted y at x = 0.

Equation

ŷ = b₀ + b₁x

     Σ (xᵢ − x̄)(yᵢ − ȳ)
b₁ = ────────────────────
        Σ (xᵢ − x̄)²

b₀ = ȳ − b₁x̄

Same five points

b₁ = 8 / 10 = 0.8
b₀ = 3 − 0.8 × 3 = 0.6

If y is log₁₀ flow:
  +0.8 in log₁₀ = ×10⁰·⁸ = ×6.3 in cfs
b₁ uses the same sum as r: 8. Dividing by Σ(x − x̄)² instead puts it in units of y per x.
Part 1 · Concept 6 of 7

r², the Share of Spread Explained

Definition

r² is the share of the spread in y that the line accounts for, from 0 to 1.

Equation

r² = 1 − SSE / SST

SST = Σ (yᵢ − ȳ)²
SSE = Σ (yᵢ − ŷᵢ)²

Same five points

SST = 10.0   misses from the flat mean
SSE =  3.6   misses from the line

r² = 1 − 3.6 / 10
   = 0.64
   = 0.80²
The line removes 6.4 of the 10 units of squared spread.
Part 1 · Concept 7 of 7

RMSE, the Typical Miss

Definition

RMSE, root mean squared error, is the typical size of a residual, in the units of y.

Equation

RMSE = √( (1/n) Σ (yᵢ − ŷᵢ)² )

     = √( SSE / n )

Same five points

RMSE = √(3.6 / 5)
     = √0.72
     = 0.85

If y is log₁₀ flow:
  ×10⁰·⁸⁵ ≈ ×7 in cfs
Most points fall within one RMSE of the line. r² is a share; RMSE is a size, in y's units.

02 · Correlation and its traps

One Number, Three Different Stories

Air temperature and dew point

r = 0.97

Shared physics: warm air holds more water. Strong, and no discovery.

Same-day rain and river flow

r = 0.11

Weak: the river answers rain days later, and most days have no rain.

7-day rain and log10 flow

r = 0.31

Same two records, chosen with physics: three times the correlation.

02 · Correlation and its traps

A Third Variable: the Season

1Monthly rain vs monthly flowr = 0.43
2After removing each month's normalr = 0.27

Both records rise in spring and fall in late summer. Part of their correlation comes from that shared calendar, not from one driving the other day by day.

Removing each month's normal (Week 6 anomalies) takes out the season and shrinks r. What is left is closer to the rain-to-river link itself.

Left: mean of each calendar month, 2019–2025, rain at Iowa City (top) and Iowa River discharge (bottom).

Pause and think

?

Ice cream sales and drownings rise and fall together. What drives both, and what plays that role for rain and river flow?

Summer heat drives both: a confounder, the classic third variable
The season lifts rain and flow together; removing it takes monthly r from 0.43 to 0.27

03 · Least squares on our data

Reading the Line in Flow Units

Python

a, b = fit["intercept"], fit["slope"]
print(f"dry week:       {10 ** a:,.0f} cfs")
print(f"+25.4 mm rain:  x {10 ** (b * 25.4):.2f}")
print(f"+50 mm rain:    x {10 ** (b * 50):.2f}")
print(f"typical miss:   x {10 ** fit['rmse']:.1f}")

Output

dry week:       881 cfs
+25.4 mm rain:  x 1.62
+50 mm rain:    x 2.57
typical miss:   x 3.1
  • A model fitted in log10 adds in log10, so it multiplies in cfs
  • Each answer is a factor, never "plus so many cfs"
  • The typical miss (×3.1) is larger than the effect of an inch of rain (×1.6)

04 · Rebuild, step 1 of 3

Give the Model the River's Memory and Scale

s = merged.sort_values("date").set_index("date")
s["precip_7day_mm"] = (s["precipitation_mm"]
                       .rolling("7D", min_periods=7).sum())
s["log10_discharge"] = np.log10(s["discharge_cfs"])
s = (s.dropna(subset=["precip_7day_mm", "log10_discharge"])
      .reset_index())

fit = linear_regression_summary(s["precip_7day_mm"],
                                s["log10_discharge"])

Fixes 1 and 2. Same two files; r² rises from about 0.01 to 0.099. Both choices came from physics before the fit: rivers integrate rain over days, and flow varies by factors.

Fixed: the river's memory and its skew

05 · Residuals

Three Shapes Residuals Take

Random scatter

The line took what a line can. The rest is noise or missing variables.

A curve

The relationship bends: a straight line is the wrong shape.

A funnel

Spread grows with the fitted value. A log transform usually cures it.

Our data, 7-day rain fit: in raw cfs the residual spread grows from 1,466 to 2,997 cfs from the lowest to the highest third of fitted values, a funnel. In log10 units it stays between 0.44 and 0.53.

05 · Residuals

Our Residuals Follow the Seasons

+Spring and early summer: more flow than the rain predictsJun +0.42
−Late summer and autumn: lessSep −0.47

+0.42 in log10 units is a factor of 2.6; −0.47 is a factor of 2.9 the other way.

The same week of rain does more in May than in September. Wet soils send rain to the river; dry soils and growing crops keep it. One rain predictor cannot hold that, so the residuals do.

06 · Honest evaluation

Why Split by Time, Not at Random

1Today vs yesterday, log10 flowr = 0.99
2Today vs a week agor = 0.90

Each day is almost a copy of its neighbours. Draw test days at random and nearly every one has its yesterday and tomorrow in the training set: the test is not new data.

Our two-parameter line has little to memorise, so a random split scores about the same (0.503). A flexible model would not; the time split stays honest either way.

06 · Honest evaluation

How Good Is a Test RMSE of 0.467?

An error means little until it is set beside something simple. Our model beats the flat line by 0.047 and the monthly normals by 0.028. Yesterday's flow is 7 times better: the river's own memory outpredicts rain. The model describes a relationship; it is not a forecast.

06 · Uncertainty

How Sure Are We of the Slope?

Python

lo = fit["slope"] - 1.96 * fit["stderr"]
hi = fit["slope"] + 1.96 * fit["stderr"]
print(f"slope   {fit['slope']:.4f} log10 cfs per mm")
print(f"95% CI  {lo:.4f} to {hi:.4f}")
print(f"p       {fit['p_value']:.1e}")
print(f"r²      {fit['r_squared']:.3f}")

Output

slope   0.0082 log10 cfs per mm
95% CI  0.0072 to 0.0092
p       2.5e-59
r²      0.099
  • A tiny p and a small r² are both true: a precise estimate of a weak effect
  • The interval assumes independent days; at r = 0.99 day to day they are not
  • So treat 0.0072 to 0.0092 as the narrowest the truth could be, not the range

07 · Pitfalls

Where This Week Goes Wrong

  • Slope reported in cfs when it lives in log10 space
  • r² of 0.10 called "useless", or "proof", without a baseline beside it
  • Rain window chosen by peeking at the test years
  • The largest residuals deleted instead of looked up
  • A random train/test split on daily data

Summary

Key Takeaways

  • Look at the distribution first; it chooses the units of the model
  • Correlation screens; the season and other third variables can produce it
  • A slope fitted in log10 is a factor in cfs
  • Residual structure is missed science: here, the seasons
  • Score on unseen years, beside a simple baseline
  • Suggests, shows, predicts, proves: pick the verb the evidence earns
