Look First

Before Any Fit: the Histogram

Daily discharge

  • Strongly right-skewed
  • Most days quiet, a few days flood
  • Mean pulled far above the median

The consequence

  • A line fit in raw units chases the floods
  • log10 makes multiplicative behavior linear
  • This is why the lab transforms before fitting

Correlation

Correlation and Its Traps

  • Temperature vs dew point: r near 0.9, shared physics
  • Precipitation vs discharge: both cycle with seasons
  • A third variable can drive both sides
  • Correlation screens candidates; mechanism makes claims

Least Squares

What the Fitted Line Minimizes

log10(Q) = a + b * P7 (minimize sum of squared misses)
Q = daily discharge (cfs)
P7 = trailing 7-day precipitation (mm)
b = slope: log10 cfs per mm of weekly rain
a = intercept: log10 flow at zero recent rain
  • Vertical misses, squared: big errors dominate the fit
Earth & Environmental Computing | Week 08 | 7
Least Squares

Why 7-Day Rain and Log Flow?

Physical reasoning

  • Rivers integrate rain over days: sum a week
  • Flow varies multiplicatively: take log10
  • Transforms chosen BEFORE fitting

The payoff

  • Daily rain vs flow: weak r-squared
  • 7-day vs log flow: substantially stronger
  • The improvement confirms the reasoning
Earth & Environmental Computing | Week 08 | 8
Least Squares

Reading r-squared Honestly

  • Fraction of variance the line explains
  • 0.4 on daily environmental data can be genuinely informative
  • 0.99 in a field this noisy is grounds for suspicion
  • r-squared says fit, never cause

Residuals

Residuals Are the Model's Confession

Random scatter

Around zero: the line captured what a line can.

Curvature

The relationship is not linear; the model misses structure.

Funnel

Variance grows with fitted value; log transform usually helps.

Big isolated residuals are events or data problems: investigate, never delete silently.
Earth & Environmental Computing | Week 08 | 10
Residuals

The Two Diagnostic Panels

fitted = a + b * sample["precip_7day_mm"]
residuals = sample["log10_discharge"] - fitted

ax1.scatter(fitted, residuals)      # structure = missed physics
ax1.axhline(0)
ax2.hist(residuals, bins=40)        # symmetric? heavy-tailed?
  • Left panel: look for curvature, funnels, stripes
  • Right panel: errors roughly symmetric around zero?

Concept Check

Residuals fan out at high fitted values. Which regression assumption failed, and does the log transform help or hurt?

Constant variance failed (heteroscedasticity, in the textbook's word)
Log compresses the high end: usually exactly the cure
Earth & Environmental Computing | Week 08 | 12
Honest Evaluation

Split by Time, Not at Random

train: 2019-2023 | test: 2024-2025
fit parameters on train only
score RMSE on both periods
random splits leak tomorrow into today
adjacent days are not independent
  • Parameters always flatter the years that chose them; the test years are the honest score

Worked Example

The Lab's Regression, End to End

Given

Merged daily weather and flow, 2019-2025; 7-day rain sums and log10 flow.

Solve

Fit on 2019-2023, apply to 2024-2025, report RMSE for both periods in log10 units.

Interpret

Similar errors: the relationship generalized. Much worse on test: it memorized.

Language

Calibrated Claims

  • This fit SUGGESTS a rain-flow relationship
  • The residual pattern SHOWS missing seasonal structure
  • Nothing here PROVES cause
  • The lab write-up is graded on calibration as much as computation

The Regression Dictionary, Decoded

  • A tiny p-value and a modest r-squared coexist happily
  • With thousands of days, even weak relationships are detectable

Outliers

Investigate, Never Silently Delete

The five largest residuals

  • Look up their dates first
  • Snowmelt? Upstream release? Sensor glitch?
  • Each hypothesis is checkable

The rule

  • Deletion changes the fit AND the story
  • Document any exclusion with a reason
  • An unexplained outlier is a finding, not dirt
