Core Concept

Dates Done Right, from the First Line

weather = pd.read_csv(path, parse_dates=["date"])

weather["date"].dt.year      # 2019 ... 2025
weather["date"].dt.month     # 1 ... 12
weather["date"].dt.dayofyear # 1 ... 366
  • parse_dates turns strings into timestamps at read time
  • The .dt accessor exposes calendar parts as columns

Core Concept

Groupby: Split, Apply, Combine

stream.groupby(stream["date"].dt.month)["discharge_cfs"].mean()
split = rows into 12 month-groups
apply = mean within each group
combine = a 12-row table
answers: what is a TYPICAL May?
  • Every May in the record contributes to the May number

Core Concept

Resample: Calendar-Aware Aggregation

monthly = (weather.set_index("date")
           .resample("MS")
           .agg({"temp_mean_c": "mean",
                 "precipitation_mm": "sum"}))
  • Needs a datetime index: set_index first
  • Knows February is short; no fixed-window trick does
  • Answers: what happened in May 2019, specifically?
SEES:4100 | Week 06 | 8
Core Concept

Groupby Month vs Resample Month

groupby(month)

  • 12 rows, the climatology
  • A typical year, month by month
  • Every May averaged together

resample("MS")

  • One row per actual month
  • The historical sequence
  • May 2019 stands alone
Conflating these two is the most common error of the week.

The Science

Aggregation Is a Scientific Choice

  • Monthly temperature: a mean
  • Monthly precipitation: a sum
  • Monthly discharge: a mean of a rate
  • A monthly mean of daily rainfall answers a different question

Core Concept

Rolling Windows and Anomalies

s = stream.set_index("date").sort_index()
s["rolling_30d"] = s["discharge_cfs"].rolling(30, min_periods=15).mean()

clim = s.groupby(s.index.month)["discharge_cfs"].mean()
s["anomaly"] = s["discharge_cfs"] - s.index.month.map(clim)
  • Rolling smooths storms without snapping to calendar edges
  • Anomaly = observed minus what is normal for that month

Core Concept

Merging Two Records

merged = weather.merge(stream[["date", "discharge_cfs"]],
                       on="date", how="inner")

len(weather)   # 2019-2025 weather days
len(stream)    # 2015-2025 flow days
len(merged)    # the overlap only
  • Inner join keeps dates present in BOTH tables
  • len() before and after, every merge, every time

Core Concept

Date Arithmetic for Event Windows

event = pd.Timestamp("2024-06-18")
window = flow_by_date.loc[event : event + pd.Timedelta(days=5)]

window.iloc[0]              # flow on the rain day
window.max()                # peak within five days
window.to_numpy().argmax()  # days until that peak
  • Timedelta makes calendar windows explicit
  • This exact pattern builds the lab's event-response table
