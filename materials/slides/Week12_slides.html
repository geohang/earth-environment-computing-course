The Model

The Bucket, Drawn Before Coded

Flows

  • Precipitation in at the top
  • Evapotranspiration out
  • Quickflow spills from surplus
  • Baseflow leaks through the bottom

Two knobs

  • k_runoff: fraction of surplus to quickflow
  • k_baseflow: fraction of storage leaked per day
  • Every code line = an arrow on this drawing
SEES:4100 | Week 12 | 4
Vocabulary

Inputs, States, Parameters

Inputs

Time series the world provides: rain, potential evapotranspiration.

States

What the model remembers between days: storage in the bucket.

Parameters

Numbers we choose or fit: the two k values.

Keep these three straight and any environmental model becomes readable.
SEES:4100 | Week 12 | 5
The Model

One Day of the Bucket

available = storage + max(precip, 0)
actual_et = min(max(pet, 0), available)
surplus   = max(precip - actual_et, 0)
quickflow = k_runoff * surplus
storage   = max(available - actual_et - quickflow, 0)
baseflow  = k_baseflow * storage
flow      = quickflow + baseflow
  • Seven lines of mass accounting
  • Water is conserved: nothing appears, nothing vanishes

Units

The Price of Admission: Real Units

runoff_mm_per_day = Q_m3s * 86.4 / area_km2
Q = discharge (m3/s), from the USGS record
area = 3,271 sq mi = about 8,470 km2 at Iowa City
86.4 = seconds per day / 1e6 * 1000
result: same unit as precipitation
  • Model and observations must speak mm/day before any comparison

Calibration

Calibration Is a Search

  • No handbook lists your watershed's k values
  • Try parameter pairs, score each against observed flow
  • The lab's grid search: 320 model runs, fully transparent
  • Silent assumptions: observations trustworthy, structure adequate
SEES:4100 | Week 12 | 9
Scoring

Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency

NSE = 1 - sum((obs - sim)^2) / sum((obs - mean(obs))^2)
NSE = 1: perfect match
NSE = 0: you tied the mean
NSE < 0: the mean beats your model
the rival is always: just predict the mean
  • Expect modest or negative validation NSE this week, and say so out loud

Honest Testing

Calibrate on Year 1, Judge on Year 2

The split

  • Grid search sees days 1-365 only
  • The model then runs both years
  • NSE reported separately per year

Reading the pair

  • Validation below calibration: expected
  • Validation far below: memorized the year
  • Validation above: get suspicious, check the split

Equifinality

The Valley in the Calibration Surface

  • Many parameter pairs fit almost equally well
  • Different internal stories, same hydrograph
  • A good fit cannot certify the mechanism
  • Report ranges; validate on unseen data; prefer measurable parameters

Missing Processes

What Iowa Does That the Bucket Cannot

  • Stores precipitation as snow, releases it in spring
  • Freezes the ground: infiltration shuts off
  • Grows corn: water use peaks in July
  • One added snow store would fix the biggest miss

Inputs

The PET Proxy, and Its Honesty Problem

PET = 0.12 * max(T_mean, 0) [mm/day]
T_mean = daily mean temperature (deg C)
0.12 = a crude coefficient, not a law
monotonic with temperature: the right shape
ignores radiation, humidity, wind, crops
  • One of the model's largest input uncertainties, and Friday's sensitivity practice: change 0.12 to 0.20 and watch the calibration shift
SEES:4100 | Week 12 | 16
Reading States

The Storage Series Is a Sanity Check

  • Fills in fall and winter, draws down through summer
  • If storage grows without bound, a leak term is broken
  • If it pins at zero all year, ET or runoff is eating everything
  • Plot the state, not just the output: models fail visibly there first
