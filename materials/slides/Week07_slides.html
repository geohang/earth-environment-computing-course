02 · Anatomy of a figure

Two Ways to Call Matplotlib

pyplot state machine

plt.plot(s["date"], s["discharge_cfs"])
plt.ylabel("Discharge (cfs)")
plt.title("Iowa River, 2019")
plt.show()

Acts on whichever axes is "current". Fine for a quick look; ambiguous once there are two panels.

Explicit figure and axes · use this

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 4))
ax.plot(s["date"], s["discharge_cfs"])
ax.set_ylabel("Discharge (cfs)")
ax.set_title("Iowa River, 2019")

Every call names its target. The same code scales to two, four or nine panels without changes.

02 · Anatomy of a figure

Multi-Panel Layouts

fig, (ax_rain, ax_flow) = plt.subplots(
    2, 1,
    sharex=True,
    figsize=(10, 6),
    gridspec_kw={"height_ratios": [1, 2]},
    layout="constrained",
)
  • 2, 1: two rows, one column; returns two axes you can name
  • sharex=True: one time axis, so dates line up straight down
  • height_ratios: the effect (river) gets twice the height of the cause (rain)
  • layout="constrained": matplotlib spaces labels so panels do not overlap
  • figsize is in inches; 10 × 6 fits a slide or a full-width page

Critique and Rebuild

03 · Live critique

Find Five Problems

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(3, 2))
ax.plot(stream["date"], stream["discharge_cfs"])
ax.plot(weather["date"],
        weather["precipitation_mm"])
plt.show()

Same 2019 data as the rest of this section. Work in pairs for two minutes.

03 · Live critique

Five Problems, Named

1No axis labels or units: the reader cannot check a single value
2Rain (mm) and flow (cfs) share one axis, so rain flattens to zero
3No legend: which line is which?
43 × 2 inch canvas: ticks are unreadable once printed
5No stated message: nothing tells the reader where to look
03 · The checklist

Non-negotiable Elements

01

Labels + units

Discharge (cfs). A figure without units cannot be checked.

02

Readable size

Text legible from the back row, or in a two-column PDF.

03

Legend

Whenever two lines share a panel. No guessing.

04

Stated message

Title or caption says what to see, not just what it is.

This checklist is the figure rubric for every remaining lab.

03 · Rebuild, step 1 of 3

Two Panels, One Time Axis

w = weather[weather["date"].dt.year == 2019]
s = stream[stream["date"].dt.year == 2019]

fig, (ax_rain, ax_flow) = plt.subplots(
    2, 1, sharex=True, figsize=(10, 6),
    gridspec_kw={"height_ratios": [1, 2]})

ax_rain.bar(w["date"], w["precipitation_mm"],
            width=1)
ax_flow.plot(s["date"], s["discharge_cfs"])

Fixes problem 2. Rain is daily totals, so it is drawn as bars; flow is continuous, so it is a line.

03 · Rebuild, step 2 of 3

Labels, Units, Inverted Rain

ax_rain.invert_yaxis()
ax_rain.set_ylabel("Rain (mm/day)")
ax_flow.set_ylabel("Discharge (cfs)")
ax_flow.set_xlabel("Date, 2019")

ax_flow.xaxis.set_major_formatter(
    mdates.DateFormatter("%b"))
ax_flow.yaxis.set_major_formatter(
    StrMethodFormatter("{x:,.0f}"))

Fixes problems 1 and 4. Month names replace ISO dates; thousands separators make 12,000 readable at a glance.

03 · Rebuild, step 3 of 3

One Highlight, One Annotation

flood = (pd.Timestamp("2019-05-17"),
         pd.Timestamp("2019-07-04"))
for ax in (ax_rain, ax_flow):
    ax.axvspan(*flood, color="0.92", zorder=0)

peak = s.loc[s["discharge_cfs"].idxmax()]
q = peak["discharge_cfs"]
ax_flow.annotate(f"Peak {q:,.0f} cfs, 29 May",
    xy=(peak["date"], q), xytext=(20, 0),
    textcoords="offset points",
    arrowprops={"arrowstyle": "-"})

Fixes problem 5. The band marks 17 May to 4 July, when flow rose and stayed above 9,000 cfs.

05

Color

A colormap is an axis. Choose it the way you choose a scale.

05 · Color

Rainbow Versus Sequential

jet

jet, in grayscale

viridis

viridis, in grayscale

  • jet's bright cyan and yellow bands invent edges the smooth field does not have; in grayscale its order is lost
  • viridis gets brighter from low to high, so the order survives printing and most color-vision deficiencies
05 · Color

Three Kinds of Colormap

TypeUse when the data areExample in our datamatplotlibMap
SequentialMagnitudes from low to highElevation (m), temperature (°C)viridis, YlGnBu
DivergingDepartures from a meaningful centerTemperature anomaly from the site meanRdBu_r, centered on 0
QualitativeUnordered categoriesStation, land-cover classtab10
AvoidNever for quantitative dataIt invents boundariesjet, rainbow
05 · Color

Diverging Maps for Anomalies

grid = temp.pivot(index="y_km", columns="x_km",
                  values="temperature_c")
anom = grid - grid.values.mean()
lim = np.abs(anom.values).max()

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(7, 5))
im = ax.pcolormesh(
    grid.columns, grid.index, anom,
    cmap="RdBu_r", vmin=-lim, vmax=lim)
fig.colorbar(im, ax=ax, label="Anomaly (°C)")
ax.set_aspect("equal")
ax.set_xlabel("x (km)")
ax.set_ylabel("y (km)")

Captions and Saving

07 · Captions

The Three-Part Caption

What

Daily discharge of the Iowa River at Iowa City for 2019, with daily precipitation inverted above.

How

USGS station 05454500 and Iowa Environmental Mesonet data; highlighted band marks the spring flood, 17 May to 4 July.

Notice

The river peaked at 12,600 cfs on 29 May, one day after 42.7 mm of rain; flow stayed above 9,000 cfs into early July.

Pause and think

?

A caption reads: "Figure 1. Discharge versus time." Which of the three parts is present, and what is missing?

Only "what", barely: no how, no notice
A reader learns nothing they could not see already
07 · Captions

From Caption to 200-Word Interpretation

01 · Pattern

What the eye should find

Flow rose from about 2,000 to 12,600 cfs over two weeks of repeated storms.

02 · Mechanism

The physical reason

214 mm fell between 17 and 29 May on already wet soils, so most rain became runoff.

03 · Caveat

One honest limitation

Rain is from one station, IOW; the Iowa River drains a much larger basin upstream.

07 · Saving

Saving Figures Like a Professional

from earthcourse.plotting import save_figure

saved_path = save_figure(
    fig,
    PROJECT_ROOT / "figures"
    / "grouplab01_rain_river_2019.png",
)
print(saved_path)
  • Creates figures/ if it does not exist
  • Saves at 150 dpi with bbox_inches="tight", so labels are never clipped
  • The saved file is the deliverable; name it like you mean it: lab, content, year
  • Reference the filename in your caption and README

08 · Pitfalls

Where Group Figures Go Wrong

  • Module C waits for final data instead of designing with placeholders
  • Over-decoration: gridlines and colors that add nothing
  • Captions that describe axes instead of stating the finding
  • A boring year chosen when a flood year was available
Summary

Key Takeaways

  • One figure, one claim, said in one sentence
  • Work through fig, ax; every element has a method
  • Labels, units, legend, size: the non-negotiables
  • Log axes when data span orders of magnitude
  • Sequential for magnitude, diverging for anomalies, never rainbow
  • Captions: what, how, notice
