# Computational Methods for Earth and Environmental Science

**Format:** 16-week course

**Audience:** Students new to scientific programming

This course introduces Python programming, reproducible workflows, data
processing, visualization, algorithm development, introductory statistics,
geostatistics, and simple environmental modeling. The examples use Earth,
environmental, hydrologic, climate, geophysical, and geospatial questions.

## Weekly course structure

Each teaching cycle begins with concepts and live coding. The next meeting
starts with a 10-minute student-led debrief of the previous lab, followed by the
new lab. A studio session provides time to work, test, and ask questions.

Beginning with Lab 2, each student completes a short debrief card before the
next lab meeting. The card records a solved problem, an unresolved problem
with evidence, or a design and verification choice. Students are evaluated on
preparation, clear evidence, and constructive participation.

## Suggested teaching sequence

| Week | Topic | Lab or project work |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Course overview and reproducible workflows | Individual Lab 1 |
| 2 | Python basics | Individual Lab 2, Part A |
| 3 | Conditionals, loops, and events | Individual Lab 2, Part B |
| 4 | Functions and debugging | Individual Lab 4 |
| 5 | NumPy and arrays | Individual Lab 5 |
| 6 | Pandas and time series | Individual Lab 6 |
| 7 | Scientific visualization | Group Lab 1 |
| 8 | Statistics and regression | Group Lab 2 |
| 9 | Algorithms and interpolation | Group Lab 3 |
| 10 | Responsible AI-assisted coding | Group Lab 4 |
| 11 | Geostatistics and spatial prediction | Group Lab 5 |
| 12 | Simple environmental modeling | Group Lab 6 |
| 13 | Final project launch | Proposal workshop and studio |
| 14 | Reading week or academic break | No new lab |
| 15 | Advanced applications and project support | Project studio |
| 16 | Course synthesis | Final presentations |

Individual Lab 2 spans Weeks 2 and 3 as one notebook with two graded
checkpoints. That structure suits a calendar in which Week 3 loses a
meeting to a holiday, and it keeps each concept session in the same week
as the lab work it supports.

## Grading weights

- Individual labs: 30%
- Group labs: 25%
- Final group project: 30%
- Individual contribution statement and participation: 10%
- Attendance, lab-debrief participation, and in-class coding engagement: 5%

## Lab submission structure

Each student lab contains guided examples, a graded checkpoint requiring new
code or analysis, a written scientific explanation, and a debrief card. An
unchanged guided notebook does not satisfy the graded checkpoint.

## Responsible AI-assisted coding

When allowed, students may use AI coding tools for debugging, refactoring,
comments, tests, and documentation. Students must disclose meaningful AI
assistance, review and run all code, verify outputs, and be able to explain
submitted work. AI tools may not be used to fabricate data, citations, results,
or interpretations. Restricted data may not be uploaded to an AI service.
