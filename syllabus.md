# SEES:4100 Computation in the Earth and Environment

**Term:** Fall 2026  
**Credit:** 3 semester hours

This course introduces Python programming, reproducible workflows, data
processing, visualization, algorithm development, introductory statistics,
geostatistics, and simple environmental modeling. The examples use Earth,
environmental, hydrologic, climate, geophysical, and geospatial questions.

## Weekly course structure

Monday introduces the next method through lecture and live coding. Wednesday
begins with a 20-minute student-led debrief of the previous lab, followed by the
new lab. Friday is an open lab and studio period.

Beginning with Lab 2, each student completes a short debrief card before the
Wednesday meeting. The card records a solved problem, an unresolved problem
with evidence, or a design and verification choice. Students are evaluated on
preparation, clear evidence, and constructive participation.

## Grading weights

- Individual labs: 30%
- Group labs: 25%
- Final group project: 30%
- Individual contribution statement and participation: 10%
- Attendance, lab-debrief participation, and in-class coding engagement: 5%

## Lab submission structure

Each student lab contains guided examples, a graded checkpoint requiring new
code or analysis, a written scientific explanation, and a debrief card. An
unchanged guided notebook does not satisfy the graded checkpoint.

## Responsible AI-assisted coding

When allowed, students may use AI coding tools for debugging, refactoring,
comments, tests, and documentation. Students must disclose meaningful AI
assistance, review and run all code, verify outputs, and be able to explain
submitted work. AI tools may not be used to fabricate data, citations, results,
or interpretations. Restricted data may not be uploaded to an AI service.
